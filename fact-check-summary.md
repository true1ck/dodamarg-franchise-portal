# Franchise Investment Plan Fact Check

This is a source-based audit of the most material claims in `Dodamarg Franchise Business Investment Master Plan.md`.

## Bottom line

The plan is not reliable as a financial source.

Many brand names are real, but the setup costs, royalty claims, staffing assumptions, and break-even figures are often:

- materially lower than the brand’s public page;
- based on dealer/partner/reseller models rather than franchises;
- or not publicly supported at all.

The monthly profit and break-even numbers are mostly marketing-style estimates and are not verifiable from public brand sources.

## Brand-model mismatches

These are not just “cost” issues. The plan also describes the wrong business structure in some cases.

- `Piramal Sarvajal` is described like a paid franchise, but the official site explicitly warns against investment-based purifier distribution schemes.
- `Printo` is presented as a franchise, but the public offer I found is a reseller program.
- `Urban Company` is a service-professional partner network, not a retail franchise format.
- Several EV and solar rows are really dealer, partner, or host models, not micro-franchises with a published low entry ticket.

## Verified examples

These rows were broadly consistent with public sources:

- `T VANAMM` - official site says ₹3.5L all-inclusive and zero royalty.
- `Zorko` - official franchise pages say around ₹9L–₹10L and no royalty / no profit sharing.
- `Amul Preferred Outlet` - official franchise page supports a low-entry outlet model, though the exact amount in the plan is a bit high.
- `Tea Time` - real brand, but the plan’s amount is slightly off.

## Finance and education highlights

The finance and education blocks also lean heavily on partner/agent models, and the public numbers usually do not match the plan:

- `Kidzee` public franchise page says about ₹15L–₹16L and a 2000–3000 sq. ft. requirement.
- `UCMAS` and `Brainobrain` are real education brands, but the public pages I found do not support the specific tiny-ticket franchise numbers in the plan.
- `Angel One` and `Sharekhan` use authorised-person / partner models rather than a simple retail franchise model.
- `SBI`, `PNB`, and `BOI` business correspondent / CSP programs are bank-agent models, not traditional franchises.
- `Vakrangee` does have kiosk/franchise material, but the plan’s figures are not cleanly supported by the public documents I found.
- `Thyrocare` and `Pathkind` both have real partner/franchise pages, but the plan’s cost figures are off; Pathkind’s official page shows a minimum investment of ₹45,500 plus infrastructure, while Thyrocare’s public pages include much smaller entry points for specific models.

## What this means for the file

The document should not be treated as a vetted investment guide.
It is better described as a promotional shortlist with many unsupported figures.

If you want to make it dependable, the next step is to replace every row with one of these labels:

- `verified from official source`
- `partially verified`
- `unsupported`
- `wrong`

Then add one source link per row and remove all profit and break-even numbers unless they are explicitly published by the brand.
